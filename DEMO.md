# Screen demo · strictly 2:00

**On screen throughout:** `1101-hub.github.io/guided-airdrop`

About 275 spoken words plus three short silences — **2:00** exactly at 150 words a minute — so keep a little pace in hand.
Every number below is what the page will actually show **if you follow the actions exactly and
touch nothing else.**

**Before you record**
- Hard-refresh (`Ctrl+Shift+R`) so you're on the newest build.
- Zoom ~90% so the canvas and the sliders are both in shot.
- **Do not move a single slider first.** All the numbers assume the defaults.
- Close other tabs — the tab bar is in shot.

> **The rule:** press the button, shut up, let it land, *then* talk. Three seconds of silence
> feels like forever to you and reads as confidence to everyone else.

**SAY**
> July 2026. A cloudburst floods four districts of Upper Assam. Seven lakh people affected,

## 0:13 — 0:32 · One drop

**SHOW** Scroll to **01 · the toy**. Press **▼ drop it**.

**SAY**
> So: how accurately can you drop a box of food?

`— 3 seconds. Say nothing. Let it land. —`

> Forty metres up. Top, the side view. Bottom, the same flight from above. Missed by **two
> point three metres** — and it wandered on the way down. Remember that.

## 0:32 — 0:50 · Take the wind away from it

**SHOW** Click the first option card, **"Nothing at all."** Then press **▼ drop it**.

**SAY**
> Same drop, one change: this one doesn't know the wind exists.

`— 3 seconds. Let it land. —`

> **Twenty-three point nine metres.** Same wind, same height, same parachute. The only
> difference is whether it knows what the air is doing — twenty-one metres worse.

---

## 0:50 — 1:12 · Three at once, then fifty

**SHOW** Press **race all three**. `— let them land —` Then press **50 drops at once**.

**SAY**
> All three together. Pink knows nothing. Blue works it out itself — that's mine. Green is
> handed the true wind, which is cheating.
>
> One drop is luck. Fifty drops, randomised: median **nine point eight metres** knowing
> nothing, **three point six** working it out.

---

## 1:12 — 1:42 · Why it has to turn

**SHOW** Scroll to **05 · no wind sensor on board**. Click **force it straight**.

**SAY**
> No wind sensor on board. GPS gives speed over the ground, the maths gives speed through the
> air — the difference is the wind. But flying straight, a headwind and flying slower look
> **identical**.
>
> Forced straight: **two point eight metres per second** wrong.

**SHOW** Click **let it turn**. The solid lines snap onto the dashed ones.

> Let it turn: **four centimetres**.
>
> Turning breaks the tie: the wind holds still while your own speed swings round. And it was
> already turning. **The move that fixes the timing is the move that finds the wind.**

---

## 1:42 — 2:00 · The finding, and the honest bit

**SHOW** Scroll to **07 · the finding**.

**SAY**
> Heavier flies faster but turns wider. Too light and the wind takes it. The rule that falls
> out: **double the wind you design for, four times the landing error.**
>
> I'm not the first to build one — what's new is the rule. Live at **1101-hub dot github dot
> io slash guided-airdrop.**

## Do not say

- **Don't call the fifty-drop medians the paper's numbers.** The paper's table is a separate
  150-drop run over a different wind range. On screen it is fifty — say fifty.
- **Don't promise the "too slow to fly" pink band.** At the default 1.6 m/s wind it never
  appears; the wind has to be above about **2.1 m/s** before any part of the slider turns pink.
  To show it you'd push wind to **3.0** and drag weight below **0.53 kg/m²** — not worth the
  seconds in a two-minute cut.
- **Don't claim it beats being handed the true wind.** Green wins on screen, and the paper says
  those two aren't separated at 150 drops. If asked, the defensible line is: *drop for drop it
  cuts the miss by 4.26 m and wins 112 times out of 150.*

---

## If you overrun

Cut the fifty-drop line at **0:50** and keep only *race all three*. Never cut the turning
section at **1:12** — it is the one genuinely clever thing in the project, and it is what makes
this more than a simulation.
