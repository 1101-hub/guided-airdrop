# Screen demo · strictly 2:00

**On screen throughout:** `1101-hub.github.io/guided-airdrop`

The shape: **it fails → why that matters → it works → why it works → proof → the rule.**
You open on a failure and explain nothing for thirteen seconds. Let the miss do the talking.

## Set up before you press record

1. Hard-refresh the page (`Ctrl+Shift+R`).
2. Zoom ~90% — canvas and sliders both in shot. Close other tabs, the tab bar is visible.
3. Scroll to **01 · the toy**.
4. **Click the first option card — "Nothing at all."** Leave every slider alone.
5. *Now* start recording.

> **The one rule:** press, silence, land, *then* speak. Never talk over a flight.
> Three seconds of quiet feels endless to you and reads as confidence to everyone else.

## Why it's built this way

- **You open on the failure.** Nobody needs briefing to understand a box landing in the wrong
  place. Explanation lands better once someone already wants the answer.
- **The wandering is planted at 0:00 and paid off at 1:20.** That's the one moment that turns
  this from a simulation into an idea — so it gets the callback, and it gets the most time.
- **Say a number only when it *is* the point.** Twenty-four, two point three, four centimetres.
  Everything else is on screen already; reading it aloud only slows you down.
- **Never describe what's visible.** No "top panel, side view". If a picture needs narrating,
  fix the picture instead.

---

## Do not say

- **Don't call the fifty-drop medians the paper's numbers.** On screen that's a 50-drop run; the
  paper's table is a separate 150-drop one over a different wind range. The script says "around
  ten" and "under four" for exactly that reason — honest roundings of what's on screen.
- **Don't claim it beats being handed the true wind.** There's a third card that simply tells it
  the answer, and it does about as well. The intervals overlap, so those two are *not*
  separated. Keep it out of the video; have the answer ready for Q&A.
- **Don't promise the "too slow to fly" pink band.** At the default 1.6 m/s wind it never
  appears — the wind has to be above about 2.1 m/s before any of the slider turns pink.

## If they ask in Q&A

**"Isn't the version that's told the wind better?"**
> On the median, slightly — but the error bars overlap, so at a hundred and fifty drops those
> two aren't separated. What I *can* defend is the paired comparison: drop for drop, working out
> the wind cuts the miss by four point two six metres, and it wins a hundred and twelve times
> out of a hundred and fifty.

**"Has nobody done this before?"**
> They have. The Naval Postgraduate School lands one within three metres, and ParaDrone flies
> these paths on a hobby microcontroller. What neither of them publishes is how wing loading and
> wind together set the floor on accuracy. That's the part that's mine.
