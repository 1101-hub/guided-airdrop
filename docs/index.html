02 · the one number that decides everything

try dragging it

How heavy you make it.

Too light and the wind blows it away. Too heavy and its turns get too wide to fix anything at the end.

v = √(2mg / ρSCL) Weight over canopy area is the only thing that sets how fast it flies.

Left of the pink line it cannot fly into the wind at all. No amount of clever steering fixes that — it is simply slower than the air.

04 · the shortest way round

drag the arrow

Only six shapes exist.

It cannot turn on the spot, so it can rarely fly straight at the target.

In 1957 Lester Dubins proved the shortest path is always one of six shapes. An infinite question becomes picking the smallest of six numbers.

05 · no wind sensor on board

press the buttons

It works out the wind by turning.

GPS says how fast you cross the ground. The maths says how fast you move through the air. The difference is the wind.

But flying straight, a headwind and simply flying slower look identical. Turning tells them apart — the wind keeps pointing one way while you swing round.

Why the last row is not a win

Row 2 looks better than row 3, but the error bars overlap, so those two are not really separated at 150 drops. The honest claim is the paired one: drop for drop, working out the wind cuts the miss by 4.26 m, and wins 112 times out of 150.

What this leaves out

No gusts, no canopy flexing, no servo lag. These numbers are a best case. The model names a test build — 1 m² canopy, 0.5–0.8 kg — so a real flight can prove it wrong.

Not the first guided parafoil

The Naval Postgraduate School's Snowflake lands within 3 m, and ParaDrone already flies Dubins paths on a hobby microcontroller. No priority is claimed over either. What is new here is the design analysis they do not publish.

07 · the finding

Double the wind, four times the error.

To fly in wind W you need weight going up as W². And heavier means wider turns.

Rmin ∝ W² Verified to 14 decimal places — an identity of the model, not a fitted curve.

So building for wind you never meet costs you accuracy all the time, even on a still day.
