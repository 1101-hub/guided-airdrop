 WHAT CHANGED FROM v1
   v1 was a good demo and a bad explanation.
   It never said you built a model, never said
   how a parachute can steer at all, and its
   two best moments (the wandering, and the
   W-squared rule) both rested on ideas it
   never taught. All four are fixed below.

It's not a round parachute.

[ SCROLL to 03 · NO WIND SENSOR ON BOARD ]

So how does it know the wind?

GPS gives its speed over the ground, /
the maths gives its speed through the air,

Except that fails
in a straight line. /
A headwind, and just flying slower,
look identical.

Forced straight — /

[ CLICK "let it turn" ]

Because when you turn, the wind
keeps pointing one way and you don't.

 THE FOUR THINGS A JUDGE MUST LEAVE WITH
   1. She built a model of a self-steering
      relief parachute.
   2. It glides, so it can be steered — but it
      has a fixed flying budget.
   3. It finds the wind with no wind sensor,
      and only turning makes that possible —
      which it was doing anyway.
   4. Accuracy is capped by the square of the
      wind you design for.
   If any one of those is missing, the video
   has not done its job.

 NUMBERS THAT SHOULD APPEAR
   cold open drop ........... 23.9 m
   second drop .............. 2.3 m  (green, 21.6 better)
   forced straight .......... 2.82 m/s
   let it turn .............. 0.04 m/s
   50 drops ................. 9.8 m  vs  3.6 m

   If a number is different, a slider moved.
   Reload, redo the setup, start again.

 NEVER SAY
   · "these are the paper's numbers"  (on screen = 50 drops,
     the paper = a separate 150-drop run)
   · "it beats being told the true wind"  (the intervals
     overlap — it is NOT separated)
   · "nobody has done this before"  (they have — say so first)

 IF ASKED: isn't the one that's TOLD the wind better?
   On the median, a bit — but the error bars overlap,
   so at 150 drops those two aren't actually separated.
   What I can back up: drop for drop it cuts the miss
   by 4.26 metres, and it wins 112 times out of 150.

 IF ASKED: has nobody done this?
   They have. Naval Postgraduate School lands one within
   3 metres. ParaDrone flies these paths on a hobby
   microcontroller. What neither of them publishes is how
   the weight and the wind together set the limit on
   accuracy. That part is mine.

 IF ASKED: is this simulated or real?
   Simulated — it is a mathematical model, and I say so
   in the video. No turbulence, no canopy flexing, no
   servo lag, so the numbers are a best case. The model
   names a test build — 1 m2 canopy, 0.5 to 0.8 kg — so a
   real flight can prove it wrong.
