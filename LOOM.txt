 HOW TO READ THIS

───────────────────────────────  0:00
[ PRESS "drop it" ]
[ SILENCE. 3 seconds. Let it land. ]

That's a box of food dropped
out of a helicopter, /
and nothing's steering it.

It landed twenty-four metres
from where it should have.


───────────────────────────────  0:11
[ stay put · don't scroll ]

This is Assam, last July. /
Four districts went under, /
and eighty thousand people
couldn't be reached by road.

And in a flood, that means
it's in the water.


───────────────────────────────  0:24
[ CLICK 2nd card: "Works the wind out itself" ]
[ PRESS "drop it" ]
[ SILENCE. 3 seconds. ]

Now watch this one.
Nothing's changed — /
same wind, same height, same parachute.

The only difference is
this one figures out the wind
on the way down.

Two point three metres.

And it doesn't have a wind sensor.

    ( bar goes green: 21.6 m better )


───────────────────────────────  0:42
          ** THE IMPORTANT BIT **
[ SCROLL to 05 · NO WIND SENSOR ON BOARD ]
[ CLICK "force it straight" ]

So how does it know the wind?

The GPS knows how fast
it's crossing the ground. /
The maths tells me how fast
it should be moving through the air.

Take one from the other
and that's the wind.

Except that falls apart
in a straight line.

A headwind, and just flying slower,
look identical.

Forced straight, it's three metres
a second out. Useless.

[ CLICK "let it turn" ]

Now let it turn.

Four centimetres.

Because when you turn, /
the wind keeps pointing the same way
and you don't.


───────────────────────────────  1:14
[ SCROLL BACK UP to the first drop ]

And this is the bit I like.

Go back to that first drop — /
it was wandering the whole way down.

That's not it being lost. /
That's it burning off height
it doesn't need.

So it was already turning — /
already doing the thing
that finds the wind.


───────────────────────────────  1:32
[ PRESS "50 drops at once" ]

One drop could be luck. /
So here's fifty, everything randomised.

Ten metres out without the wind,
under four with it.


───────────────────────────────  1:42
[ SCROLL to 07 · THE FINDING ]

And what I found is this. /
Build it for twice the wind, /
and your landing gets four times worse.
Even on a still day.

I'm not the first to build
a steerable parachute. /
But that bit is mine.

Go play with it —
1101-hub dot github dot io
slash guided-airdrop.

 NUMBERS THAT SHOULD APPEAR
   cold open drop ........... 23.9 m
   second drop .............. 2.3 m  (green, 21.6 better)

   let it turn .............. 0.04 m/s
   50 drops ................. 9.8 m  vs  3.6 m

   If a number is different, a slider moved.
   Reload, redo the setup, start again.

 NEVER SAY
   · "these are the paper's numbers"  (on screen = 50 drops,
     the paper = a separate 150-drop run)
   · "it beats being told the true wind"  (the intervals
     overlap — it is NOT separated)
   · "nobody has done this before"  (they have — say so first)

 WHY IT'S IN THIS ORDER
   · You open on the failure. Nobody needs briefing to
     understand a box landing in the wrong place.
   · The wandering is planted at 0:00 and paid off at 1:14.
     That is the moment it stops being a simulation.
   · Only three numbers are said out loud. Everything else
     is on screen — reading it aloud just slows you down.
   · Never describe what is visible. If a picture needs
     narrating, fix the picture.

 IF YOU RUN LONG
   cut the 50 drops at 1:32.
   NEVER cut 0:42 or the callback at 1:14.

 IF ASKED: isn't the one that's TOLD the wind better?
   On the median, a bit — but the error bars overlap,
   so at 150 drops those two aren't actually separated.
   What I can back up: drop for drop it cuts the miss
   by 4.26 metres, and it wins 112 times out of 150.

 IF ASKED: has nobody done this?
   They have. Naval Postgraduate School lands one within
   3 metres. ParaDrone flies these paths on a hobby
   microcontroller. What neither of them publishes is how
   the weight and the wind together set the limit on
   accuracy. That part is mine.
