         ** THE STEERING MATHS **
[ SCROLL to 02 · IT ISN'T A NORMAL PARACHUTE ]

It's a parafoil — a wing. /
It glides forward, so pulling one side
turns it.

Its sink rate is constant. /
So the moment it's dropped, its time
in the air is fixed — and so is the
distance it can fly. That's its budget.

The target is nearer than that, /
so it has flying to waste.

Fly at an angle alpha off the line

So set cos alpha to distance over budget. /
That is the steering law.

[ SCROLL to 03 · NO WIND SENSOR ON BOARD ]
[ CLICK "force it straight" ]

There's no wind sensor. /
Ground velocity is air velocity plus wind,
so wind is ground minus air.

GPS gives the ground part, /
the glide equation the air part.

But in a straight line, a headwind and
a slower airspeed are the same vector.

Forced straight — /

[ CLICK "let it turn" ]

Turning separates them: the wind stays put,
your airspeed rotates with you.

Four centimetres.

───────────────────────────────  1:36
        ** WHERE THE LIMIT COMES FROM **
[ SCROLL to 06 · THE FINDING ]

Lift equals weight. /
Solve for speed, and only mass over area
survives — the wing loading.

Turn radius goes as speed squared, /
speed as the root of loading —
so radius goes straight as loading.

To fly in wind W you need speed above W, /
so loading grows as W squared.

Put them together: /
the tightest turn you can make
grows as W squared.

Twice the wind, four times the error.

 THE FOUR PIECES OF MATHS IN THE VIDEO
   1. cos alpha = d / s
      Sink rate is constant, so time aloft and
      path length are fixed at release. Flying
      at alpha to the target stretches the path
      by 1/cos alpha, so alpha is set from the
      ratio of distance to budget.
   2. wind = ground velocity - air velocity
      GPS gives the first, the glide equation
      the second. In a straight line a headwind
      and a low airspeed are the same vector —
      unobservable. Turning separates them.
   3. v = sqrt(2mg / rho S C_L)
      Lift equals weight; everything cancels
      except mass over area.
   4. R = v^2 / g tan phi, with v > W
      so R_min grows as W squared.

   Each is on screen while you say it:
   1 in section 02, 2 in 03, 3 and 4 in 06.
   If a judge cannot repeat any of them back,
   the video has not done its job.

 IF ASKED: isn't the one that's TOLD the wind better?
   On the median, a bit — but the error bars overlap,
   so at 150 drops those two aren't actually separated.
   What I can back up: drop for drop it cuts the miss
   by 4.26 metres, and it wins 112 times out of 150.

 IF ASKED: has nobody done this?
   They have. Naval Postgraduate School lands one within
   3 metres. ParaDrone flies these paths on a hobby
   microcontroller. What neither of them publishes is how
   the weight and the wind together set the limit on
   accuracy. That part is mine.

 IF ASKED: is this simulated or real?
   Simulated — it is a mathematical model, and I say so
   in the video. No turbulence, no canopy flexing, no
   servo lag, so the numbers are a best case. The model
   names a test build — 1 m2 canopy, 0.5 to 0.8 kg — so a
   real flight can prove it wrong.
