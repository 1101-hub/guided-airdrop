 BEFORE YOU HIT RECORD

 ONE RULE
   press · silence · let it land · THEN talk


───────────────────────────────  0:00
[ PRESS "drop it" ]
[ SILENCE. 3 seconds. Let it land. ]

That's a box of food,
dropped from a helicopter.
Nobody steering it.

It missed by TWENTY-FOUR METRES.


───────────────────────────────  0:13
[ stay where you are · don't scroll ]

July 2026.
Four districts of Upper Assam go under.

Seven lakh people affected.
Eighty thousand unreachable by road.

Everything they get comes from the air.

A box twenty-four metres off
is a box in the river.


───────────────────────────────  0:28
[ CLICK 2nd card: "Works the wind out itself" ]
[ PRESS "drop it" ]
[ SILENCE. 3 seconds. ]

Same wind.
Same height.
Same parachute.

One difference —
this one works out the wind
on the way down.

TWO POINT THREE METRES.

And there's no wind sensor on board.

    ( bar goes green: 21.6 m better )


───────────────────────────────  0:46
          ** THE IMPORTANT BIT **
[ SCROLL to 05 · NO WIND SENSOR ON BOARD ]
[ CLICK "force it straight" ]

So how does it know the wind?

GPS tells it how fast
it's crossing the ground.

The maths tells it how fast
it moves through the air.

Subtract, and that's the wind.

Except in a straight line that fails.

A headwind — and simply flying slower —
look EXACTLY THE SAME.
There's no way to tell them apart.

Forced to fly straight,
it's nearly three metres a second wrong.

[ CLICK "let it turn" ]

Now let it turn.

FOUR CENTIMETRES.

Turning breaks the tie,
because the wind keeps pointing one way
while you swing round.


───────────────────────────────  1:20
[ SCROLL BACK UP to the first drop ]

Look again at that first flight.
It wandered the whole way down.

That wasn't waste —
it was burning off spare height.

THE MOVE IT ALREADY HAD TO MAKE
IS THE MOVE THAT FINDS THE WIND.


───────────────────────────────  1:33
[ PRESS "50 drops at once" ]

One drop is luck.

So — fifty. Wind, height and compass
error randomised every time.

Around ten metres out knowing nothing.
Under four working it out.


───────────────────────────────  1:45
[ SCROLL to 07 · THE FINDING ]

And there's a rule underneath it.

Build for twice the wind,
and the landing gets FOUR TIMES worse.

Every drop. Even on a still day.

I'm not the first to build one of these.
The rule is mine.

Fly it yourself:
1101-hub dot github dot io
slash guided-airdrop.

───────────────────────────────  2:00
                                  END

 NEVER SAY
   · "these are the paper's numbers"  (on screen = 50 drops,
     the paper = a separate 150-drop run)
   · "it beats being told the true wind"  (the intervals
     overlap — it is NOT separated)
   · "nobody has done this before"  (they have — say so first)

 IF YOU RUN LONG
   cut the 50 drops at 1:33.
   NEVER cut 0:46 or the callback at 1:20.

 IF ASKED: isn't the one that's TOLD the wind better?
   On the median, slightly — but the error bars overlap,
   so at 150 drops those two aren't separated.
   What I can defend: drop for drop it cuts the miss by
   4.26 metres and wins 112 times out of 150.

 IF ASKED: has nobody done this?
   They have. Naval Postgraduate School lands one within
   3 metres. ParaDrone flies these paths on a hobby
   microcontroller. What neither publishes is how wing
   loading and wind set the floor on accuracy.
   That part is mine.
